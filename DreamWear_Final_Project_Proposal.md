## Project Description

*DreamWear* is a two-part thesis project that transforms spoken imagination into wearable reality. The first component, *Spoken Wardrobe*, is an interactive installation where users verbally describe their dream clothing and watch as AI generates a 3D garment that appears on their body in real-time through augmented reality. The second component takes selected pieces from the collective archive of generated clothing and fabricates them into physical garments using digital manufacturing techniques.

The installation leverages a sophisticated pipeline of AI technologies: speech recognition captures the user's voice and transcribes their description, body tracking maps their physical form, and generative AI creates both a 2D visualization and a complete 3D mesh of the described garment. Through real-time pose estimation, the virtual clothing aligns with the user's body as they move, creating the illusion of actually wearing their imagination.

Each interaction contributes to a growing database—a collective dream wardrobe capturing the fashion desires of individuals unconstrained by practicality, manufacturing limitations, or commercial viability. This archive becomes both a research artifact and a source for the project's culmination: a curated collection of physically fabricated garments born from collective imagination, turning dreams into tangible reality.

The project exists at the intersection of speculative design, participatory art, and fashion technology—asking what happens when we remove all barriers between imagination and creation.

## Central Question

**What would a clothing collection made by regular people, with no limitations imposed by viability or reason, look like?**

[IMAGE 2: Central question slide from presentation with geometric design]

Fashion trends have historically flowed downward from designers to consumers. The industry operates within constraints—manufacturing capabilities, material costs, commercial appeal, and the practical demands of wearability. These constraints shape not only what gets produced but what people believe is possible to imagine.

My project inverts this paradigm by asking: *What would happen if anyone could become their own fashion designer, uninhibited by the constraints imposed onto normal fashion design and allowed to express their imagination to its fullest?*

This question unfolds into several interconnected inquiries:

1. **On Creative Expression**: When people are told they can describe *anything* they want to wear, what do they actually say? Do they describe variations of existing fashion, or do they venture into the truly fantastical?

2. **On Collective Imagination**: What patterns emerge when hundreds of people contribute to a shared archive of dream clothing? Are there common threads that reveal something about our collective desires?

3. **On the Gap Between Dream and Reality**: When AI interprets spoken descriptions into visual form, what gets lost or transformed in translation? How do people react when they see their imagination rendered visually?

4. **On Materialization**: When we take virtual clothing born from pure imagination and attempt to fabricate it physically, what challenges arise? What does the process of making dreams tangible reveal about the relationship between the virtual and the physical?

My project addresses these questions through direct experience. *Spoken Wardrobe* gives anyone the opportunity to express what they would want to wear with no limitations on viability or realism. They can then "try on" the clothing in AR and see what their imagination looks like on their own body. The resulting archive provides data to analyze patterns in imaginative clothing. The physical fabrication phase tests whether dream-born designs can exist in reality, and what transformations occur when they do.

## Motivation

I have been deeply interested in fashion to the point that I think about people's outfits almost every moment of my life. Walking through the city, I find myself studying what people wear, asking questions that never seem to leave my mind: What influences people's outfits? What allows them to turn something so ordinary in our society into something that represents them and their creativity? Why do some people dress boldly while others conform? What would those conformers create if they felt truly free?

These questions led me to a deeper one: How could this creative aspect found in people be accentuated if they had no constraints on what they could wear?

Traditional fashion design requires years of training, access to materials, knowledge of construction techniques, and usually significant financial resources. Even with these skills, designers work within the constraints of what can be physically manufactured and what will sell. For everyday people, expressing fashion creativity is limited to selecting from what already exists in stores or online—choosing from a menu written by others.

I have always wanted to design clothing for people to express themselves more clearly. But I realized something more interesting might happen if I stepped back from being the designer myself and instead created a system that allows others to design. Not by teaching them pattern-making or garment construction, but by simply asking them to speak their imagination aloud.

The voice as an input modality is crucial to this project. Speaking is natural, immediate, and unconstrained by technical skill. When you type a description, you edit yourself. When you sketch, you're limited by drawing ability. But when you speak, particularly in the intimate context of an installation that's listening just to you, something different happens. The words come from a more unfiltered place.

Giving people the firsthand ability to become their own fashion designers—and then to see the clothing become visible on their bodies in real-time—could be the first step toward understanding what fashion could be if it weren't bound by the traditional gatekeepers. And ultimately, fabricating selected pieces into physical garments closes the loop between imagination and reality, proving that dreams can indeed become wearable.

## Intended Audience

This project is for anyone. It does not matter age, gender, nationality, or background in fashion or technology. The installation is designed to be approachable by anyone willing to express their ideas out loud and who has a want to see their imagination realized.

However, the project speaks particularly to several groups:

**The Fashion-Curious but Not Fashion-Trained**: People who have opinions about what they would like to wear but lack the vocabulary or skills to participate in fashion design. The voice interface removes the need for technical knowledge—you don't need to know what a "princess seam" is or how to sketch a silhouette.

**The Imaginatively Constrained**: Those who have internalized the limitations of conventional fashion and haven't allowed themselves to dream beyond what's available in stores. The prompt to describe "anything you want to wear" gives permission to think without boundaries.

**Children**: Young people who haven't yet learned what's "impossible" in fashion often produce the most inventive descriptions. Their contributions to the archive offer a window into imagination uncorrupted by commercial fashion's constraints.

**Researchers and Designers**: The archive of generated clothing, complete with original voice transcriptions and resulting 3D meshes, provides a dataset of imaginative fashion that doesn't currently exist. Fashion researchers, AI researchers, and designers may find value in analyzing patterns of collective desire.

**The General Public at Exhibitions**: The installation will be encountered in gallery and exhibition contexts where passersby may engage without prior knowledge of the project. The experience is designed to be self-explanatory and rewarding even for someone who spends only a few minutes with it.

I will engage my target audience throughout development through iterative playtesting. The installation has already been tested at ITP, gathering feedback that has shaped the interaction flow. During the Spring semester, I will specifically seek out participants from outside the ITP community to ensure the experience translates beyond our particular context.

## Context

My work is informed by multiple domains: AI-powered fashion tools, speculative design, digital fabrication, and interactive art installations. Below I describe the projects, artworks, and literature that have shaped my thinking.

### AI Fashion Tools

**AI Outfit Generator** and **Wardrobe AI** represent the current commercial landscape of AI-assisted fashion—tools that help users mix and match existing clothing or visualize items on their bodies. These applications work within the bounds of what already exists, helping users navigate choices rather than enabling creation. My project departs from this paradigm by generating entirely new garments from verbal descriptions rather than reorganizing existing options.

**Vyu AI Clothing Try-On Mirror** offers AR visualization of existing clothing, allowing customers to see how specific garments might look before purchase. While the technology is similar to what I employ, the purpose differs fundamentally. Vyu solves a retail problem; *Spoken Wardrobe* enables creative expression.

**GENEE AR Mirrors** create immersive AR experiences in retail environments, demonstrating the viability of real-time body tracking and virtual overlay at installation scale. Their work validates the technical approach while operating in a commercial rather than artistic context.

### Speculative Design and Fashion

**Speculative Everything: Design, Fiction, and Social Dreaming** by Anthony Dunne and Fiona Raby provides the theoretical grounding for my approach. Their framework of using design to ask "what if?" questions rather than solve practical problems aligns directly with my central question. The fabricated garments from the collective archive will themselves be speculative objects—clothing that exists not because it was commercially viable but because someone dreamed it.

**Not a Toy: Fashioning Radical Characters** documents how fashion can embody fantasy, identity, and imagination beyond practical function. The characters created through radical fashion serve as precedent for the kind of imaginative freedom I'm asking participants to access.

**Virtual Clothing** and **Generative AI in Fashion** (industry research) document the current state of AI-generated fashion, providing context for what existing tools can and cannot do. Understanding these capabilities helps me push beyond current limitations.

### Digital Fabrication and Wearable Technology

[IMAGE 3: Kinematic Dress by Nervous System - black 3D-printed flowing dress]
[IMAGE 4: Second image of Kinematic Dress render]

**Kinematic Dress by Nervous System** is perhaps the most direct precedent for the fabrication phase of my project. Their approach—using computational design to create complex, flexible forms that would be impossible with traditional manufacturing—demonstrates that 3D meshes can become wearable garments. The tessellated, interlocking structure of their dress translates a digital form into a physical object that moves with the body. I will study their techniques closely when moving from generated mesh to physical fabrication.

**CuteCircuit's Wearable LED Display Dress** shows how technology can be integrated into garments that remain wearable and beautiful. Their work suggests that the boundary between the virtual and physical in fashion is already becoming porous.

**Made in Code: Reimagining the Experience of Fashion** documents how computational approaches are transforming fashion design, providing historical context for my work.

### Technical Precedents

The technical implementation draws on established libraries and approaches:

- **OpenAI Whisper** for speech-to-text transcription
- **BlazePose and BodyPix** for body tracking and segmentation
- **Stable Diffusion** for 2D image generation
- **Rodin API** for text/image-to-3D mesh conversion
- **Three.js** for real-time 3D visualization in browser

These technologies have been combined in novel ways for this specific application—the full pipeline from voice to AR visualization appears to be unique to this project.

## Form

*DreamWear* takes two distinct forms across its two phases.

### Part 1: Spoken Wardrobe (Interactive Installation)

[IMAGE 5: Installation photos showing person interacting with screen displaying "WELCOME TO SPOKEN WARDROBE"]
[IMAGE 6: Second installation photo showing generated clothing on user]

The installation consists of a large display screen, depth-sensing camera (OAK-D Pro), and microphone, creating an experience that resembles a magic mirror. Users approach and see themselves on screen. The interface guides them through the interaction:

1. **Calibration**: The system measures ambient noise to set voice detection thresholds
2. **Body Detection**: BlazePose tracks the user's skeleton
3. **Voice Prompt**: When the user speaks, their words are captured and transcribed
4. **A-Pose Capture**: A 3-second countdown prompts the user to hold a neutral pose while their body is photographed and segmented
5. **Generation**: AI generates a 2D image of clothing based on their description, then converts it to a 3D mesh
6. **AR Visualization**: The mesh appears overlaid on the user's body in real-time

[IMAGE 7: Playtest photos showing before/after of user with generated chef outfit - apron, hat]
[IMAGE 8: Multiple angles of 3D generated mesh from playtest]

The entire process takes approximately 2-3 minutes. Throughout, the interface provides visual feedback—audio level indicators, progress animations, countdown timers—keeping the user engaged during generation.

[IMAGE 9: Screenshots of real-time AR overlay showing 3D mesh following user movement with coordinate readouts]

A critical technical challenge was making the generated mesh follow the user's body convincingly. Using BlazePose landmarks (shoulders, hips), the system calculates position, scale, and rotation transforms that update at 30 frames per second. Exponential smoothing reduces jitter while maintaining responsiveness. The result is a generated garment that appears to be worn by the user as they move.

Each session saves:
- The original camera frame
- Body segmentation mask
- Generated 2D clothing image
- 3D mesh (GLB format)
- Metadata (transcription, timestamp, generation parameters)

This creates the archive that feeds into Phase 2.

### Part 2: DreamWear (Physical Collection)

[IMAGE 10: Project roadmap slide showing flow from Spoken Wardrobe → Mesh Dataset → Filter/Analyze → Digital Fabrication → Display]

The second phase transforms selected virtual garments into physical objects through digital fabrication.

**Curation**: From the collected archive, I will analyze patterns and select pieces that represent the range of imagination expressed—some that push toward the impossible, some that reveal common desires, some that are simply beautiful.

**Fabrication**: Following approaches pioneered by Nervous System's Kinematic Dress, selected meshes will be processed for 3D printing. This will require:
- Mesh preparation and repair
- Conversion to interlocking panel systems for flexibility
- Material selection for wearability
- Assembly and finishing

**Display**: The final collection will be exhibited alongside documentation of its origin—the voices that spoke these garments into existence, the AI interpretations, and the journey from imagination to physical form.

### Technical Architecture

The pipeline integrates multiple AI systems:

```
Voice Input → Whisper Transcription → Text Prompt
                                           ↓
Camera Frame → BodyPix Segmentation → Body Mask
                                           ↓
                    [ComfyUI + Stable Diffusion SDXL Inpainting]
                                           ↓
                              2D Clothing Image
                                           ↓
                         [Rodin API 3D Generation]
                                           ↓
                              3D Mesh (GLB)
                                           ↓
BlazePose Landmarks → Mesh Calibrator → Transform
                                           ↓
                    [Three.js Real-time Visualization]
```

The system runs cross-platform (Mac with Metal/MPS, PC with CUDA) and includes a WebSocket-based browser UI for flexible deployment.

## Personal Evaluation Rubric

I will consider *DreamWear* successful based on the following criteria:

### For the Installation (Spoken Wardrobe)

**Engagement Without Explanation**: Users should be able to approach the installation and complete the full interaction without external guidance. The interface must be self-explanatory. If I find myself repeatedly explaining how to use it, the design has failed.

**Imaginative Range**: The archive should contain a genuine diversity of descriptions—not just variations on "a red dress" or "a blue jacket" but explorations into the fantastical, the absurd, the deeply personal. If participants only describe conventional clothing, the prompt framing or experience design needs improvement.

**Emotional Response**: I want to see genuine reactions when people see their described clothing appear on their bodies. Surprise, delight, laughter, contemplation—any strong response indicates the experience is resonating. Indifference would indicate failure.

**Robust Operation**: The technical pipeline must function reliably across different body types, lighting conditions, and voice characteristics. Edge cases should fail gracefully rather than crashing.

### For the Collection (DreamWear)

**Wearability**: At least some fabricated pieces should be genuinely wearable—able to be put on a human body and worn, even if only for a photoshoot rather than daily life.

**Fidelity to Source**: The physical garments should maintain a recognizable relationship to their digital sources. Viewers should be able to see the connection between the generated mesh and the fabricated object.

**Narrative Coherence**: The exhibition should successfully communicate the journey from voice to fabric. Viewers should understand that these objects began as spoken words.

### Personal Growth

**Interaction Design Mastery**: I want to learn to create intuitive interaction pipelines that guide users without external help. This project forces me to think carefully about every step of the experience.

**Technical Integration**: Successfully combining speech recognition, computer vision, generative AI, and real-time graphics represents a significant technical challenge. Completing this integration will significantly expand my capabilities.

**Understanding Collective Imagination**: The archive will provide genuine data about what people dream of wearing. I want to gain insight into patterns of desire that aren't visible through traditional fashion research.

## Conclusion

*DreamWear* asks a simple question—what would you wear if you could wear anything?—and builds the technology to make the answer visible and tangible. The project lives in the space between imagination and reality, using AI not as a replacement for human creativity but as an amplifier of it.

By collecting hundreds of spoken dreams and materializing a selection into physical form, the project creates something that has never existed: a fashion collection authored by collective imagination, unconstrained by the traditional limits of the industry. What emerges may be beautiful, absurd, impractical, or surprisingly conventional—and that discovery is the point.

The installation invites everyone to be a designer. The collection proves that dreams can become real. Together, they suggest a future where the boundary between wanting and having, between imagining and wearing, becomes ever more porous.
